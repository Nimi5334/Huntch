# Huntch — Go-To-Market & Marketing Strategy
**Beachhead:** Carmel Center (מרכז הכרמל), Haifa
**Operator model:** Solo founder · ~₪0–500/mo marketing budget · sweat equity
**Last updated:** 2026-06-22
**Status:** Pre-launch. Research-backed GTM. Companion to `HUNTCH-STRATEGY.md` (product/company) and `HUNTCH-PRODUCTION-PLAN.md` (build).

> This document answers two questions only: **(1) how do we get hospitality workers to join Huntch, and (2) how do we get business owners to adopt it and keep using it for matching.** Everything is tailored to a solo founder, near-zero budget, launching in Carmel Center, Haifa. All major moves are grounded in the research summarized in the Appendix.

---

## 0. Executive summary — the core bet

Huntch is a two-sided local hiring marketplace, and the research is unanimous that hourly-labor marketplaces live or die on **liquidity in one geography before anything else**. For a solo founder with no ad budget, the winning move is not mass enrollment of either side — it is **founder-brokered, concierge liquidity in a single Carmel Center café cluster**:

> Recruit a small **starter bench** of workers → sign a handful of cafés on a **free pilot** → **hand-match every early gap yourself** → convert that proven trust into paying monthly subscriptions. Automate nothing and expand nowhere until that one cluster is liquid.

Two structural advantages make this winnable in Haifa specifically: **WhatsApp-native onboarding with no app download** (every competitor forces an app), and a later **Arabic-language supply lever** that no incumbent has (deferred to phase 2, used when expanding down to Hadar / the lower city).

The single biggest risk is **disintermediation** — a café that finds a good worker rehires them directly. The defense is built into the product strategy (free bench manager + one-tap rebooking + repeat-coverage focus), not bolted on later.

**North-star metric:** time-to-fill after a gap is reported. Everything else serves that number.

## 1. Where we are & the hard constraints

- **Product:** Clickable mockup complete (Flow 2: post gap → ranked local shortlist → invite → responders → pool). Real matching engine. Not yet: live WhatsApp, DB, payments, legal layer. (See `HUNTCH-PRODUCTION-PLAN.md`.)
- **Operator:** One person (Nimrod). Every hour spent on marketing is an hour not spent building. The plan must be ruthlessly time-efficient and front-load only what compounds.
- **Budget:** ~₪0–500/mo. This rules out paid acquisition as the *engine*. The engine is organic + physical + community + referral + founder field-sales. Paid (Click-to-WhatsApp) is a **scale lever for later**, once revenue exists.
- **Legal (non-negotiable):** Israel's **Amendment 40** makes unsolicited commercial WhatsApp/SMS a *criminal* offense — explicit prior consent required, statutory damages up to **₪1,000 per message**. Therefore **100% opt-in, inbound-initiated outreach only.** This is also a trust moat if done right.
- **Ethical (non-negotiable):** Never charge workers. Never cold-contact. No scraping of social networks or job boards.

## 2. The sequencing decision (which side first)

**Verdict: seed a small worker bench and sign cafés *simultaneously* in one cluster, hand-broker every match, and gate all growth on *paid, fulfilled* shifts.**

The reasoning, from the research:

- Gig/hourly labor marketplaces are **supply-constrained** — ~⅔ of failed marketplaces die on the supply side (a16z); NfX assigns the supply constraint specifically to short-tenure/gig work; Lenny's, Casey Winters concur. *Instinct: recruit workers first.*
- **But mass-enrolling workers first is a trap for a no-budget solo founder.** Workers disengage at ~70% within 30 days if they get no offer; Amendment 40 forbids messaging them anything they didn't ask for; and a worker who opts in and hears nothing is a destroyed trust relationship — and trust *is* the product.
- **Resolution — "enroll just ahead of demand."** Workers are the hard side to *start*; paid café shifts are the hard side to *sustain*. So you keep a thin, fresh bench ready and pour your energy into making cafés post real, paid gaps that reliably fill. The milestone that matters is **fulfilled paid matches**, not signups.

This is the literal cold-start every analog ran: Instawork founders signed up workers at restaurant back doors and matched by hand; DoorDash founders were the only drivers; Airbnb founders door-knocked hosts. **Do things that don't scale until the manual loop reliably clears.**

## 3. Two structural unfair advantages

1. **WhatsApp-native, zero app download.** ~99% of Israelis use WhatsApp (>90% daily); SMBs run their entire business on it. Every direct competitor (GetJob ~20k workers, Merge, Xtras, Job2day) is **app-based** — they impose a download on both sides. Huntch's headline on both sides: *"הכל בוואטסאפ — בלי להוריד אפליקציה."*
2. **Arabic supply lever (phase 2).** The Arab-Israeli hospitality workforce is large and underserved, and every incumbent is Hebrew-only. Carmel Center itself is Hebrew-dominant (hence Hebrew-first now), but when the cluster is liquid and you expand down to **Hadar / Wadi Nisnas / the lower city**, bilingual Hebrew+Arabic opt-in unlocks a worker pool nobody else competes for. Hold this in reserve as a deliberate expansion weapon.

## 4. The beachhead — Carmel Center (מרכז הכרמל)

**The cluster:** the café/restaurant/bakery concentration along **שדרות מוריה / שדרות הנשיא** and the side streets around them (Carmel Center proper, extending toward Horev/Ahuza). Upscale, established, residential-serving venues plus Carmel hotel traffic.

**Why it fits a solo founder:**
- Walkable, concentrated enough for one person to cover door-to-door on foot.
- Established owners → **relationship- and peer-referral-driven** buying (exactly the motion a solo founder can run; see §6). Steadier venues make better, more cooperative reference accounts and cleaner case studies.
- **Worker supply nearby:** University of Haifa (up the Carmel ridge) plus residents of Carmel / Ahuza / Romema. Hebrew-dominant — consistent with Hebrew-first.
- Far less competition than Tel Aviv; **no incumbent is running this play in Haifa at all.**

**The Carmel Center adaptation (important):** upscale/residential = **lower staff turnover = fewer "quit" emergencies per venue** than a nightlife strip. Consequences baked into this strategy:
- **Recruit a wider set of venues** so the *aggregate* gap frequency is enough to keep the bench engaged and the founder learning.
- **Lean harder on the free bench manager** (standalone value that doesn't depend on a gap happening) and on **planned/seasonal hires**, not just emergencies, to stay top-of-mind between rare quits.
- **Lead the pitch with quality, fit, and time-saved** (a reliable barista who suits an upscale room), with emergency-speed as the secondary hook — the reverse emphasis from a high-turnover nightlife cluster.

**Win condition before touching a second neighborhood (hard gate):** a Carmel Center café can report a gap and reasonably expect it filled fast — a **fulfilled fill rate in the ~30–60% band** (Rothman/Thumbtack proxy), measured per shift-type. One liquid cluster beats five thin ones — premature geographic expansion is what killed Homejoy (31 cities, supply churn, negative economics).

## 5. Motion A — How workers join (supply)

### The wedge
Every existing channel (Facebook hospitality groups, WhatsApp/Telegram job groups) is **citywide, noisy, and has zero proximity filter**. Workers wade through far-away, irrelevant, often pay-opaque posts. Huntch's wedge:

> **Proximity + speed + no app + pay shown upfront.**
> *"משמרות במרחק הליכה מהבית — מקבלים הצעה רלוונטית בוואטסאפ, בלי אפליקציה, עם השכר כתוב מראש."*

This directly attacks the segment's real pains: noise, no "near me" filter, no pay transparency (a sector with a wage-violation reputation), and bidirectional ghosting (under-34s ghost ~3× more).

### Channels, ranked for a ₪0–500 budget
All organic/physical/community/referral. **No paid ads in the engine** — they're a later scale lever.

| # | Channel | Side | Why it fits a solo, no-budget founder | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **In-person enrollment at the cafés you're signing** | Supply | The venues you pitch already employ workers with friends. Enroll existing staff first — pre-vetted, and they refer. Highest-trust supply. | Founder time |
| 2 | **University of Haifa + student channels** | Supply | Course/dorm WhatsApp groups, student job boards, campus notice boards. The proximity pitch lands hardest on students wanting work near campus/home. | ~₪0 |
| 3 | **Existing job groups — as a *partner*, not a poster** | Supply | Partner with admins of Haifa hospitality WhatsApp/Telegram/FB groups (value-share / co-brand). **Never drive-by post** — it gets you banned *and* risks Amendment 40. Turn gatekeepers into distribution. | ~₪0 |
| 4 | **In-café QR (contextual placement)** | Both | Contextual QR converts ~14% vs ~0.7% for passive street stickers. "סרוק — משמרות לידך" at the staff entrance / counter, with a clear benefit. | Print only |
| 5 | **Double-sided referral** | Both | Worker→worker reward = **priority access to nearby shifts** (costs ~nothing, high perceived value) + small cash. Double-sided referrals perform ~2.4× single-sided; referral-active marketplaces see 30–60% lower blended CAC. | Reward only |
| 6 | **Google for Jobs (JobPosting schema)** | Supply | $0, free high-intent inbound, compounds over time. Every shift you post feeds it. Build the schema once. | Eng. time |
| 7 | **TikTok/IG organic (later)** | Supply + trust | "A café in Carmel filled its shift in an hour" proof content. Not direct-response — a trust multiplier. Optional until the loop works. | ~₪0 |

### Segments, in priority order
1. **Students / young adults 18–30** (University of Haifa, Carmel-area residents) — the core, addressable, proximity-motivated segment.
2. **Experienced front-of-house "career" hourly workers** — baristas/servers who fit upscale Carmel venues; quality is the demand-side selling point.
3. **Arab-Israeli workers (phase 2, Arabic)** — activated when expanding to Hadar / lower city.

**Explicitly out of scope:** undocumented / asylum-seeker back-of-house labor. Research confirms they will not join a named, phone-linked database, and targeting them creates compliance risk. Also out of scope: agency-managed imported foreign labor.

### Worker onboarding
- **60-second WhatsApp opt-in.** No CV, no account, no app. Fields: name, role(s), neighborhood, availability/shift type, languages, expected wage, **explicit revocable consent** (attorney-reviewed Hebrew), consent source recorded.
- **First offer fast.** Operational rule: never let an *active* worker go more than ~21 days without a relevant offer, or they disengage. This is the supply-retention discipline — and the reason for "enroll just ahead of demand."

## 6. Motion B — How businesses adopt *and keep using* it (demand)

### The pitch — sell the event, not the product
> *"מלצר/בריסטה עזב? תקבל/י רשימת מועמדים זמינים מהשכונה בוואטסאפ — מדורגים לפי התאמה. חינם לחלוטין בתקופת הפיילוט; אחר כך מנוי חודשי פשוט — פחות מעלות של קריאה אחת לחברת כוח אדם."*

It works because it (a) attaches to a real, recurring, painful event, (b) lives on WhatsApp where the owner already operates, (c) reverses risk via a genuinely free pilot period (not a contingency fee you can't verify), and (d) sells the thing they value most — **time saved on a task they hate.** For Carmel Center's steadier venues, co-lead with **quality/fit/reliability** ("a barista who actually shows up and suits your room"), not just speed.

**Anchor the price against what they pay today:** ~₪90–100/hr for a waiter via a manpower agency, a dishwasher costing up to ₪20k/mo in the shortage, agency placement fees of 15–20% of first-year salary, or simply a capped, under-staffed night of lost covers.

### Acquiring the first 10 owners — the Toast restaurant playbook (most directly relevant evidence)
- **Door-to-door, inside the cluster.** In-person converts ~3× remote; owners don't read email and work on-site. This is a solo founder's highest-leverage activity.
- **Open with the problem, demo on their phone in <5 minutes.** "When someone quits on you on a Friday, what do you actually do?" — then show a real ranked shortlist on the owner's own WhatsApp. That live moment is the "aha."
- **Free pilot period (risk reversal) — not freemium, not pay-per-hire.** A time-boxed free trial of full matching converts at ~15–25% for SMB; pure freemium converts ~2–5% and devalues the product; pay-per-hire is unverifiable and unenforceable (you can't confirm the hire happened or force payment, and it invites disintermediation). The free pilot itself is the risk reversal.
- **Be a local ecosystem player.** Get plugged into the cluster's suppliers and the scene; owners trust peers and locals, not vendors.

### Acquiring the next 100 — turn density + peers into a loop
- **Referral engine, built in.** ~1 in 5 restaurant deals come from referrals; referred customers are far more likely to refer again. Reward = subscription credit (e.g., a free month) for both sides; on WhatsApp the referral is a single forward.
- **Density compounds trust.** "3 cafés on מוריה already hire with Huntch" → owners adopt what neighbors use. Saturate Carmel Center to "standard tool" status before moving on.
- **Borrow incumbent trust (when ready):** POS co-marketing (Tabit sits in owners' hands daily), Wolt merchant-ops referral wedge, and the **Restaurant & Bars Association** — the staffing shortage is *their* headline issue, a natural co-marketing angle (and their annual כנס המסעדנות concentrates owners).

### Activation & retention — the real game
A hiring tool is **event-paced, not daily.** The metric that matters is **"used on the next quit," not daily logins.** Engineer the quit event as the activation mechanic (Nir Eyal's Hook Model):
1. **Trigger:** owner texts one word ("מחפש מלצר"), or Huntch proactively pings ("עדיין מאוישים במלואכם השבוע?") — critical in low-turnover Carmel Center to stay top-of-mind between rare gaps.
2. **Variable reward:** a fresh, ranked, *nearby + available* shortlist each time — unpredictable but consistently good.
3. **Investment that compounds switching cost:** every cycle the owner rates past hires, saves favorites, and builds a "my team" — so the next shortlist is better, and leaving means losing that history.

Why this matters: ~90% of users who don't engage in the first 3 days churn; hitting first value within ~14 days drives ~80%+ retention at 12 months vs 35–50% if they miss 30 days. **The first hire must succeed, fast, by hand if necessary.**

## 7. The single-player wedge — a free "bench manager"

The strongest idea in the research, and especially powerful in a low-turnover cluster:

Give every café a **free bench manager** — a one-tap way to store their trusted past workers + availability and blast a shift opening to *their own* people over WhatsApp. It is valuable to a single café **with zero Huntch network** (it digitizes the manager's existing WhatsApp-group chaos — the OpenTable pattern: a useful tool before any marketplace exists). When the café's own bench can't cover, Huntch silently extends the request to the broader local pool — the network upgrade.

This one feature does three jobs at once:
1. **Standalone demand-side value before liquidity exists** (solves the cold-start chicken-and-egg).
2. **A daily/weekly-use habit** even between quits (essential for Carmel Center's low gap frequency).
3. **Disintermediation defense** (see §8).

---

## 8. Disintermediation defense (the existential risk)

Hiring is the textbook worst case for leakage — high-value, repeat, trust-based, pre-negotiated. A café that finds a good barista simply rehires them directly. The defense is **carrots, not walls**:
- **Own the rebooking + scheduling rail:** make "rebook this worker" one tap and easier on-platform than off.
- **Anchor on repeat shift-coverage** (high-frequency, low-stakes per instance) rather than permanent placement (one-time, high-value = guaranteed leakage).
- **Reward on-platform completion** with better access/ratings for both sides.
- Never just hide phone numbers — if users still prefer going around, the platform isn't adding enough ongoing value.

## 9. Pricing & monetization

**Model: a flat monthly subscription — NOT pay-per-hire.** Pay-per-successful-hire is rejected because it is **unverifiable and unenforceable**: you cannot confirm a hire happened (the owner can simply say "didn't work out" and hire directly), you have no leverage to collect on a one-time event, and it actively *rewards* owners for routing around the platform (disintermediation — the #1 risk). A subscription ties payment to **ongoing access**, which the platform controls — don't pay, lose access to the pool and matching. It is enforceable (card on file via Stripe, service gated on payment), predictable, and builds the habit/"infrastructure" relationship that makes the product sticky. It also defuses disintermediation: you stop policing individual hires and sell continuous access instead — even an owner who hires someone directly once keeps paying for the next match.

**Two-phase rollout:**
1. **Trust-building (free pilot):** the first ~10 Carmel Center businesses get **full access free for a limited window (~2–3 months)** — long enough to witness real, fast fills and produce the case study. Free during the pilot *is* the risk reversal; the founder hand-brokers every match so it works.
2. **Monetization:** convert to a flat monthly subscription for **everyone, including the original 10.** The 10 pioneers get a **locked "founding price"** (discount held long-term) as a reward for being first and to fuel referrals. By conversion time they are already habituated and their own pool is built, so churn risk is low.

**Price anchoring:** a modest monthly subscription is trivial against the real alternative — an empty shift, a ₪90–100/hr manpower-agency call, or screening 40 strangers from a Facebook group. The barrier was never price; it is *belief that it works*. That is why the free pilot comes first.

**Validate before locking the number:** interview owners for (a) the real cost of an empty shift and (b) actual local agency fees — the two persuasion anchors and the only material gaps in the public data.

## 10. The 90-day solo-founder sequence (Carmel Center)

> Principle throughout: **do things that don't scale.** You are the matching engine until the manual loop reliably clears.

### Weeks 1–2 — Legal + groundwork (before enrolling anyone)
- Book the **Israeli privacy attorney** (₪2,000–3,000) to review opt-in language, consent storage, STOP flow. *Nothing involving real worker data starts before this.* (See `HUNTCH-PRODUCTION-PLAN.md` Phase 0.)
- Walk the Carmel Center cluster. Map every café/restaurant/bakery on מוריה/הנשיא and side streets. Note size, vibe, and likely roles.
- Draft the **opt-in copy, the door-to-door pitch script, and QR/referral copy** in Hebrew (ready-to-use assets — these can be produced as an immediate next deliverable).
- Print ~50 contextual QR cards (₪~100).

### Weeks 2–3 — Discovery, not pitching
- Visit every venue. Ask one question: *"מה היה הקושי הכי גדול בגיוס עובדים שהיה לך החודש?"* Listen. Do **not** pitch tech.
- Goal: 10–12 owners who agree to a follow-up. Identify the 3–4 most respected/connected owners (your future reference network).

### Weeks 3–4 — Seed a thin starter bench
- Enroll workers **just ahead of demand**: existing staff of cooperative venues first, then University of Haifa student channels, then partner with 1–2 hospitality group admins.
- Target a **small, fresh, reachable bench** (quality over quantity) concentrated in/near the cluster. Personal WhatsApp welcome to every opt-in within 24h.

### Weeks 4–6 — First matches, by hand
- Return to the 10–12 owners. Demo a real shortlist live on their phone in <5 min. Offer the **free pilot** (full matching, time-boxed ~2–3 months).
- When the first gap comes in: **do not run the algorithm.** Personally review the bench, call candidates first, brief the owner, be reachable during the first shift. **This match must succeed.**
- Sign 5–8 venues onto the free **bench manager** (standalone value even before they have a gap).

### Weeks 6–9 — Build the loop
- Complete ~10 matches, aim for ≥7 successful. Debrief both sides every time.
- After each success: ask the owner *"מי עוד אתה מכיר שמתמודד עם זה?"* and facilitate the warm intro. Ask the worker for ~3 friends in hospitality.
- Stand up the double-sided referral (priority-shift access for workers; subscription credit / free month for owners).
- Start the **proactive "still fully staffed?" WhatsApp cadence** to owners (top-of-mind between rare Carmel gaps).

### Weeks 9–13 — Prove the cluster, prep the gate
- Targets by day ~90: ~50 active workers, ~8 active businesses, ~10 completed matches, **1 documented case study** (the specific-crisis → specific-resolution → specific-endorser story).
- Track the **hard gate**: fulfilled fill rate trending into the 30–60% band per shift-type.
- As the free-pilot window closes, **convert the pilot businesses to the flat monthly subscription** (founding price for the first 10) — your first real willingness-to-pay signal; treat the conversion rate as a gate too.
- Only when the gate is cleared, scope neighborhood #2 (logical next: down toward Hadar / lower city — where the **Arabic lever** switches on).

## 11. Metrics that matter

- **North star:** time-to-fill after a gap is reported.
- **Liquidity (the gate):** fulfilled fill rate per cluster, per shift-type (target ~30–60%). Measure *fulfilled* matches, never signups.
- **Supply health:** % of enrolled workers who responded in the last 30 days (intervene below 40%); every active worker gets an offer within 21 days.
- **Demand habit:** repeat-event usage rate — % of owners who use Huntch on their *next* quit (the true retention metric for an event-paced tool).
- **Trust/quality:** worker show-up rate; two-sided ratings.

---

## 12. Top risks & mitigations

1. **Disintermediation (#1, existential):** subscription model — sell ongoing access, never per-hire (§9) — plus bench manager + one-tap rebooking rail + repeat-coverage focus + carrots over walls (§8).
2. **Worker churn / ghosting:** enroll just ahead of demand; offer within 21 days; reliability ratings; measure fulfilled matches.
3. **Premature expansion:** hard liquidity gate before neighborhood #2 (Homejoy's grave).
4. **Amendment 40 / consent:** opt-in only, attorney-reviewed, inbound-initiated; treat compliance as a trust moat.
5. **Low Carmel-Center gap frequency:** widen the venue base, lean on the bench manager + planned/seasonal hires + proactive check-ins, co-lead on quality not just emergency speed.
6. **Job2day** (near-identical competitor: flat ₪35/fill, ~1k workers/60 biz, Tel Aviv): out-execute on matching quality, WhatsApp-native delivery, the quit-event narrative, and own **Haifa** (and later Arabic) before they look north. Watch their traction.
